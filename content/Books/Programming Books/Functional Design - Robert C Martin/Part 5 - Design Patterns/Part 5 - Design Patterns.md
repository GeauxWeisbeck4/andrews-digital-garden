# V

# Design Patterns

The idea of design patterns[1](https://learning.oreilly.com/library/view/functional-design-principles/9780138176518/part05.xhtml#p05fn1a) was one of the most profound in the software industry. It ranks up there with structured programming, object-oriented programming, and functional programming. It told us that applications consist, in part, of repeatable and reusable elements. Those elements solved problems common to many, if not all, applications.

[1.](https://learning.oreilly.com/library/view/functional-design-principles/9780138176518/part05.xhtml#p05fn1) The definitive work on this topic was Erich Gamma, Richard Helm, Ralph Johnson, and John Vlissides, _Design Patterns: Elements of Reusable Object-Oriented Software_ (Addison-Wesley, 1994).

Of course, like all good ideas in software, design patterns have been misunderstood, overused, abused, and even discarded as archaic or specific to only very narrow contexts. This is a shame, because design patterns are eminently useful.
